The Landscape

What even is all this?

Concept 1

AI, platforms, and models — three different things

Before we get into how AI works, it helps to get oriented on the landscape — who's making these tools, what they're actually called, and why the naming is so confusing.

🔌

AI is electricity. The platform is the appliance. The model is what's inside the appliance that makes it actually work.

These three words get used interchangeably, but they mean different things.

AI is the broad capability — the underlying technology that can understand and generate language. Think of it like electricity. Electricity itself isn't a product; it's what powers products.

A platform is the product you actually use — the interface, the app, the thing you log into. ChatGPT is a platform. Claude.ai is a platform. They're the appliances plugged into the electricity.

A model is the specific engine running inside the platform — the trained system doing the actual thinking. This is where names like GPT-4o or Claude Sonnet come from. They're not products you buy; they're engines that power products.

Why it matters Why does this matter? Because when someone says "I use Claude," they mean the platform. When they say "I'm using Sonnet," they mean the model running inside it. And when they say "this is powered by AI," they mean the broad capability — which tells you almost nothing useful.

The Copilot exception — why it's confusing
🚗

Copilot is Microsoft's car with OpenAI's engine inside.

Microsoft makes the platform. But Microsoft doesn't make the model. Copilot runs on OpenAI's GPT models under the hood — Microsoft licensed the engine and built the car around it.

This is why Copilot feels similar to ChatGPT in some ways — they're running the same engine, just with different interfaces and different integrations. Copilot plugs into Office, Teams, and Windows. ChatGPT stands alone.

Concept 3

What model versions actually mean

🐎

Model versions are like horsepower ratings. More isn't always what you need — it depends on what you're trying to do.

When you see names like Haiku, Sonnet, Opus — or GPT-4o, GPT-4, GPT-3.5 — they're describing different versions within the same model family. Each version makes different tradeoffs.

Within the Claude family

Haiku

Fastest, cheapest, handles simple tasks well.

Best for: quick questions, high-volume tasks

Sonnet

The middle ground — capable and efficient.

Best for: most everyday work, coding, writing

Opus

Most capable, slowest, most expensive.

Best for: complex reasoning, nuanced tasks

The takeaway Think of it like coffee sizes, except instead of small/medium/large the tradeoffs are speed, cost, and capability — and bigger isn't always better. If you need a quick answer to a simple question, Haiku will get you there faster and cheaper than Opus would.

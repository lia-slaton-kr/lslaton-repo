Concept 1

What is an AI model, actually?

🧠

…think of it like a person who read an almost incomprehensible amount of text — books, articles, code, conversations — and got extremely good at pattern-matching what words and ideas tend to follow other words and ideas.

Most people think of ChatGPT or Claude as software, like an app. But the more useful mental model is to think of it like a person who read an almost incomprehensible amount of text — books, articles, code, conversations — and got extremely good at pattern-matching what words and ideas tend to follow other words and ideas.

When you type something to Claude, it's not "looking up" an answer the way Google does. It's doing something closer to very sophisticated autocomplete — predicting what a useful, coherent response looks like based on everything it absorbed during training.

That's important because it explains both why AI is impressive and why it sometimes confidently says wrong things. It learned patterns, not facts.

Concept 2

Platforms vs. Models — the confusion everyone has

🚗

The platform is the car. The model is the engine.

Claude is made by a company called Anthropic. But "Claude" is actually a family of different engines — Haiku, Sonnet, Opus — each with different tradeoffs between speed, cost, and capability. When someone says "I'm using Sonnet," they mean they're using a specific version of the Claude engine, usually through a platform like Claude.ai or an API.

The same logic applies across the industry:

The confusing one Microsoft Copilot is a good example of why this gets confusing — it's a platform built by Microsoft, embedded in Office tools, but it actually runs OpenAI's models under the hood. The car is Microsoft's. The engine is OpenAI's.

Concept 3

What are tokens, and why does anyone care?

🧩

AI doesn't read your text the way you do — word by word. It breaks everything into tokens, which are roughly chunks of text.

AI doesn't read your text the way you do — word by word. It breaks everything into tokens, which are roughly chunks of text. Sometimes a token is a whole word. Sometimes it's part of a word. Sometimes it's punctuation.

"Unbelievable" might be 3 tokens: Un+ believ+ able
"cat" is probably 1 token: cat
A rough rule of thumb: 1 token ≈ ¾ of a word
Why designers care Why does this matter for you as a designer? Because tokens are the unit of measurement for everything — cost, speed, and most importantly, memory.

Concept 4

The context window — the most important concept nobody explains well

🪟

Think of the context window like a whiteboard.

Everything written on the whiteboard — your prompts, the AI's responses, any documents you've pasted in — takes up space. The context window is how big that whiteboard is, measured in tokens.

When the whiteboard fills up, older stuff gets erased to make room. This is why a long conversation can feel like the AI "forgot" what you said at the beginning — it literally can't see it anymore.

This is also why pasting in a huge document at the start of a conversation matters — it takes up a big chunk of your whiteboard before you've even asked a question.

Small context window

shorter memory, cheaper, faster

Large context window

longer memory, more expensive, slower

The takeaway Different models have different sized whiteboards. This is actually one of the main things that distinguishes a "better" model from a "worse" one — not just smarts, but memory.

Concept 5

What's an MCP? (And why designers are starting to care)

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol — and it's newer, so don't worry if you haven't heard it.

📚

Think of it like giving someone a library card on top of their existing knowledge.

By default, an AI model only knows what's in its training data and what's on the whiteboard right now. It can't look things up, open your Figma file, or check your company's documentation on its own.

MCP is essentially a standardized way to give AI models access to external tools and data sources. Think of it like giving someone a library card on top of their existing knowledge.

Without MCP

the AI only knows what it learned and what you typed

With MCP

the AI can reach out and pull in live information, access files, run searches

Why designers care For designers, this starts to matter when you hear about Claude Code connecting to your file system, or AI tools that can read your design tokens or documentation. That connection is happening through something like MCP.
